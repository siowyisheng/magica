# Settle secret Bids by commit-and-reveal inside the engine

Contested Spells need both duellists to choose a Mana Bid without seeing the other's, but every client runs the whole engine and holds the whole match state, and the target is peer-to-peer play with no game server. We therefore never put a Bid into game state or the action log before both are locked in: each duellist first sends only a commitment, the SHA-256 of the Bidding id, their player id, the amount and a random 16-byte salt. Once the window closes, each reveals the amount and salt, and the engine checks them against the commitment. The commitments, the window closing and the reveals are ordinary actions, so replays, spectating and determinism are unchanged. The engine uses a small synchronous SHA-256 of its own, because `crypto.subtle.digest` is asynchronous and `applyAction` must stay synchronous and pure. Salts come from `crypto.getRandomValues` in the UI, outside `src/game/`, in the same way the match seed does.

We considered keeping Bids hidden in the UI only, which is simpler but leaks to anyone reading memory or network traffic once play is online, and an authoritative server, which costs money and isn't needed for anything else yet. The rules that make commit-and-reveal hold up are part of the design. Nothing is deducted, displayed or played until the reveal. The opponent's lock-in is never shown. The window closes as soon as both have bid, for pace. That lets someone who locks in late tell the other already has, but never what they bid. A duellist who doesn't commit or reveal in time bids 0, and an overbid counts as 0 and forfeits all of that mage's Mana.
